# Study Objective

Build a durable command of data structures and algorithms fundamentals, become
an interview-ready LeetCode problem solver for job applications, and apply the
resulting reasoning and implementation skills in day-to-day professional work.

## Intended outcomes

### Foundations

Understand the models behind core data structures and algorithms: their
invariants, correctness arguments, complexity trade-offs, and appropriate use
cases. Practice should strengthen understanding rather than become mechanical
answer collection.

### Interview-ready problem solving

Use LeetCode as structured practice for recognizing patterns, forming a
solution, communicating the approach, implementing it correctly, and checking
time and space complexity. The aim is reliable technical-interview readiness,
not a public ranking or contest reputation.

### Professional transfer

Carry these skills into professional life by choosing suitable data models,
reasoning about performance and correctness, decomposing unfamiliar problems,
and explaining technical trade-offs clearly.

## Evidence of useful progress

Useful evidence includes explaining an approach from first principles,
implementing a solution without copying it, identifying and correcting a
mistake, and applying an appropriate algorithmic idea to a real engineering
problem. Counts, streaks, and solved-problem history are private study aids;
they do not by themselves establish mastery.

## Learning-experience evidence

Each authored learning block should make the intended reasoning observable:
the learner states an invariant or contract, predicts a state transition,
traces a small example, explains a complexity claim, and attempts a closely
related task independently. The interface may provide immediate conceptual
feedback, but it must not present a completion state or correct answer as a
claim of mastery. Private responses, completion history, and review state are
study aids only and remain outside project-authored or generated public
materials.

## Boundaries

This objective governs the project’s learning direction. It does not publish
personal progress, solutions, notes, credentials, or fetched LeetCode content.
Those remain in their designated local-only paths.
